Career Inflection Points

Four moments that changed how I operate.

Not only successes, but evolution. This is probably the most personal page on the site: four moments when a belief I held about my craft was confronted by reality and had to change.

Leaving iFood to build at Porto

What I believed

I believed that building mattered more than controlling, and that accelerated growth, on its own, was the main driver of valuation. Controls and resource discipline felt secondary next to speed.

What happened

With the end of the era of "artificially cheap capital" after the pandemic, the cost of money rose and startups and scale-ups were forced to rethink their growth models, now under pressure to show efficiency, not just expansion.

What changed

The market began demanding ambidexterity: growing and innovating without giving up control and efficient use of resources. I saw that one doesn't live without the other.

How I operate today

I practice ambidextrous management: I use innovation to raise the company's ceiling, gaining scale and scope, and pair it with continuous control and efficiency, which generate the profitability needed to fund the very efforts of expansion and innovation.

Downsizing at Porto

What I believed

I saw Finance, above all, as an area to fuel growth. That, to me, was its main value proposition.

What happened

We had to abruptly shrink the Olho Mágico operation. The sharp rise in interest rates and the end of Porto's (PSSA3) Corporate Capital risk appetite for the venture made the scenario unsustainable.

What changed

The radical shift in funding availability and the steep drop in runway forced the downsizing and the layoff of 20 people. Handling that while keeping respect and clarity with those leaving and those staying was one of the hardest decisions of my career.

How I operate today

I learned that Finance is not only support for growth and innovation: it is also risk management and work with medium- and long-term scenarios. And that hard conversations are part of the seat; avoiding them costs more than facing them.

Building FP&A governance at VOLL

What I believed

I saw governance as a process restricted to internal controls, procurement, accounting, and controllership. I didn't see FP&A at the center of that conversation.

What happened

I had to run a due diligence for Warburg Pincus's evaluation to acquire VOLL, which put the quality and traceability of the financial numbers under external scrutiny.

What changed

FP&A became central: both in gathering the governance documentation and in structuring the processes that sustain it, such as management by indicators, revenue and cost audit controls, and predictability ensured by the accuracy of the numbers.

How I operate today

I constantly assess whether the chosen KPIs really move the business, and whether they are accurate, reliable, and predictable. That, together with clear, transparent governance of results and forecast data, is what gives the confidence to decide quickly.

AI and the value of my own work

What I believed

I grew professionally by producing good analyses, cross-referencing data, and generating actionable insights. I believed it was exactly that, the quality of my own analysis, that defined my value.

What happened

I found that AI does this better than I do, and faster. Acknowledging that openly is a real vulnerability: what got me here is no longer a guarantee of anything.

What changed

Instead of fighting AI, I began using it at full power, because that is value, not a threat. My focus shifted to what the machine doesn't do: uniting teams, managing priorities, and ensuring the best decisions turn into value for the company.

How I operate today

I use AI as a multiplier and concentrate my value in judgment, context, and people. And I lead teams to use more AI, be more productive, and walk the same development path I did.
